# Appendix A. Assumptions and Design Decisions

The school gave me nine class levels, an assessment structure, a grading scale and an abbreviation. Everything else in this document is a decision I made, and a decision made silently is a decision nobody can argue with later. Each entry below states what I decided, why I decided it, and what I turned down. Where the body of the document cites an entry number, it cites the numbers used here, and those numbers do not change between revisions of this document. If a decision is reversed, its entry stays and gains a note.

## A.1 Platform and access

**1. One school, one installation.** I specified a single-school build with the school identity held in settings rather than a multi-tenant product with a schools table. The school is buying software for itself, and a tenant column on every table costs the engineers real time and buys nothing this year. I rejected multi-tenancy because the migration path, if the proprietor ever opens a second branch, is a second installation, and that is cheaper than carrying the abstraction now.

**2. Privileges are flat strings and scope lives on the role assignment.** A privilege is `pupil.view` or `result.enter`, and the arms an account may touch are recorded on the assignment that granted the role. My reason is that the alternative, encoding scope into the privilege name, produces `result.enter.primary3a` and a privilege table that grows every time the school opens an arm. I rejected a hard-coded Class Teacher type with a special-cased arm field, because the school will eventually want a subject teacher who covers three arms, and a type cannot express that.

**3. The first account is created by an installer command that refuses to run twice.** It prints a password once, to the console, and forces a change at first sign-in. I chose this over a default account with a known password, which is the most common way small deployments get taken over, and over a web setup wizard, which is an unauthenticated account-creation endpoint sitting on the internet until somebody remembers to disable it.

**4. No email channel, and no self-service password reset.** Temporary passwords are generated by a Super Admin and handed to the member of staff in person, and a forgotten password is reset the same way. I made this decision because the school has no mail domain, staff use personal addresses inconsistently, and a reset link sent to an address nobody checks is a support call that ends with the proprietor telephoning me. Handing over a password in a staff room of eight people is acceptable here in a way it would not be in a larger school. I rejected transactional email, which needs a domain, a sending reputation and a monthly bill, and I rejected security questions, which are guessable by colleagues.

**5. No second factor in this version.** I decided against it because there is no verified mobile number for any account, an authenticator app that a teacher installs on a phone she later replaces produces a lockout only I can clear, and I would be introducing a failure mode more likely than the attack it prevents. I rejected optional time-based codes as well, since an optional control that the two accounts holding real privilege will not switch on is decoration. This is a stated limitation and not an oversight, and it should be revisited when the school has verified numbers.

**6. Six roles are seeded, and all six are editable.** I seeded Super Admin, Head Teacher, Class Teacher, Subject Teacher, Bursar and Records Clerk because a school of this size recognises those names, and I made them editable because the names in my head are not the names in their staff room. The only fixed thing is the `is_super_admin` flag. I rejected fixed built-in roles, which force a school into somebody else's organisation chart, and I rejected starting with no roles at all, which makes first-run configuration an hour long before anybody can enter a pupil.

**7. Sessions expire after 45 minutes of inactivity, and a privilege change ends every session for that account immediately.** Forty-five minutes is long enough to write remarks for an arm without being logged out mid-paragraph and short enough that a browser left open on a shared office machine is not an open door. I rejected a longer window because the office computer is shared, and I rejected a shorter one because losing a half-written remark to a timeout is exactly the annoyance that makes staff stop using a system.

**8. The audit write happens inside the same database transaction as the change it records.** If the audit fails, the change fails. I chose this because an audit log that is best-effort is worthless on the one day it matters, which is the day a parent disputes a mark. I rejected a message queue and a separate logging service, both of which are the right answer at a scale this school will not reach and both of which can silently fall behind.

## A.2 Configuration

**9. A published result set carries a frozen copy of the configuration that produced it.** The grading bands, the assessment weights, the school name and the head teacher's name are written into the result set as a snapshot at publication, and published sheets always render from the snapshot. My reason is that a school will change a grade boundary in March, and the First Term sheets printed in December must not silently change with it. I rejected recomputing from live settings on every render, which rewrites history, and I rejected forbidding settings changes after publication, which is not a rule any school will accept.

**10. The assessment structure locks for the whole session once the first mark is entered.** Components may be renamed and reordered at any time, but their number and their maximum marks are fixed from the first mark until the session closes. I decided this because the annual average compares three terms against each other, and three terms scored out of different structures are not comparable. I rejected locking per term, which allows a session whose First Term is out of 100 and whose Second Term is out of 120, and I rejected allowing changes with a recompute, because the marks already entered cannot be rescaled honestly.

**11. The grading scale must cover 0 to 100 with no gap and no overlap, and it is validated on save.** I chose strict validation with ten specific rejection messages over a warning, because a gap at 79.5 produces a result sheet with a blank grade in front of a parent, and by then the term is over. I rejected letting the school save an incomplete scale and flagging it at computation time, which moves the discovery of the problem to the worst possible moment.

**12. A level with only one arm still shows the arm label, so a pupil in the only arm of Primary 4 appears as Primary 4A.** I know the tidier-looking option is to print Primary 4 when there is nothing to distinguish, and I rejected it deliberately. The label would then appear on the result sheet in the year the school opens a second arm and disappear in the year it closes one, so a parent comparing two years of sheets sees a change that means nothing, and a school reprinting an old sheet gets different output than the original. A rule that produces the same string forever is worth a small loss of tidiness.

**13. Class levels are ordinary records with full editing, not values compiled into the application.** The nine levels are seed data. I made them editable because a school that adds a creche or a Primary 7 preparatory year should not need a developer, and because the chain rules can be validated at save time just as well as they can be assumed. I rejected an enumeration in code, which is faster to build and turns the first curriculum change into a deployment.

**14. An arm belongs to a session.** Primary 3A in 2026/2027 and Primary 3A in 2027/2028 are two rows, and a pupil's membership is an enrolment record with dates against one of them. My reason is that a school changes how many arms it runs from year to year, and the register of who sat in Primary 3A in 2026/2027 must stay true after the school reorganises. I rejected a single permanent arm row with a session column on the enrolment, because closing an arm then hides history, and I rejected recording the arm as a text field on the pupil, which loses the whole history the moment a pupil is promoted.

**15. Capacity is a soft limit.** The system warns when an arm is full and lets an authorised account continue, recording an override in the audit log. I chose this because a proprietor faced with a paying parent in the office will admit the thirty-first pupil whatever the software says, and a hard limit at that moment produces a workaround outside the system. I rejected a hard block, and I rejected removing capacity entirely, because the warning is useful and the override record tells the school how often it happens.

**16. One deletion rule for the whole product: delete only where nothing has ever referenced the row, otherwise deactivate.** I wrote it once, in section 9.4, and applied it entity by entity rather than deciding case by case during the build. My reason is that inconsistency here is what produces an application where deleting a subject destroys three years of marks. I rejected soft-deleting everything, which leaves a school unable to tidy up a typo made two minutes ago, and I rejected engineer's discretion, which is how the first option happens by accident.

## A.3 Sessions, promotion and pupils

**17. Sessions and terms carry explicit dates, and nothing rolls over automatically.** A new session is created by a person, and the active session is set by a person. I decided against automatic rollover on a calendar date because Nigerian resumption dates move, sometimes at short notice, and a system that opens the new session on its own will do it while the previous term's results are still unpublished. I rejected inferring the active term from today's date for the same reason.

**18. Promotion is a batch the school reviews before it commits.** The system proposes an outcome per pupil from the annual average and the core subject rule, a person edits what they disagree with, and the batch is committed as one action that can be reversed. My reason is that promotion is a judgement involving a child's age, attendance and the family's circumstances, and none of that is in the database. I rejected automatic promotion on a threshold, which would move a child on a number alone, and I rejected pupil-by-pupil manual promotion, which for nine levels is an afternoon of clicking and produces a half-promoted school if it is interrupted.

**19. The annual average is the plain average of the three term averages by default, and term weighting is an option the school must switch on.** I chose the simple average because it is what the school's paper sheets do now, because a parent can check it with a calculator, and because it does not require me to guess a weighting the school never asked for. Weighting of 20, 30 and 50 is available for schools that want the third term to count for more. I rejected making weighting the default, and I rejected averaging the twelve raw subject totals across the year, which quietly disadvantages a pupil who joined in the Second Term.

**20. Rounding is half up, and pupil figures show two decimal places while class figures show one.** I fixed this in one place because two developers will otherwise choose differently and a result sheet will disagree with a broadsheet by 0.01, which a parent will find. I rejected banker's rounding, which is correct for accounting and surprising to everybody in a school, and I rejected showing more decimals, which makes a sheet look like a laboratory report.

**21. Positions use competition ranking, so tied pupils share a position and the next position skips.** Two pupils tied at 6th are both 6th and the next is 8th. By default the sheet prints "6th (tied)", which the school can switch off. My reason is that this is what schools already do by hand, and that breaking a tie on a hidden rule such as alphabetical order invents a difference between two identical performances. I rejected dense ranking, which produces two 6ths followed by a 7th and makes the class size stop matching the last position.

**22. Bulk import is all-or-nothing.** A spreadsheet is validated in full, every problem is reported with its row and column, and nothing is written unless every row passes. I decided this because a partial import leaves the clerk guessing which forty of sixty rows arrived, and because the natural recovery, running the file again, then creates duplicates. I rejected importing the valid rows and returning an error file, which is friendlier in the first minute and worse in the next hour.

**23. Registration numbers come from a counter row updated and returned in one statement.** The system never counts existing pupils and adds one. My reason is that two clerks registering pupils at the same moment will otherwise be handed the same number, and a unique index will reject the second registration in front of a parent. There is a unique index as well, as a backstop, but it should never fire. I rejected counting rows, and I rejected a random number, which parents and teachers cannot read out over the telephone reliably.

**24. A registration number is permanent.** Correcting one is a Super Admin action with a typed reason, and the old number stays in a history table as a working lookup alias. I chose permanence because the number is written on the child's file, their books and their result sheets, and a number that changes makes those documents wrong. I rejected free editing, and I rejected refusing corrections entirely, because a clerk will eventually type the wrong admission year and the school needs a path that is auditable rather than a path through the database.

**25. The serial counter is keyed on the admission year alone, not on the year and the abbreviation.** If the school changes its abbreviation in the middle of a year, numbering continues from where it was rather than restarting at 0001. My reason is that restarting would produce two pupils whose numbers differ only in a prefix nobody remembers. I rejected keying on both, which looks more correct in a schema diagram and is wrong in the office.

## A.4 Marks, results and computation

**26. A pupil absent from an examination is ranked, but excluded from the class average, the highest and the lowest.** The sheet prints ABS in the examination column and totals the continuous assessment only. I decided the pupil must still be ranked because a position list that omits a child raises a question the school cannot answer politely, and I decided to exclude the pupil from the class statistics because a 24 counted into an average pulls the whole class down and misrepresents the teaching. The sheet therefore states the number of pupils counted alongside each class average, so the two figures are consistent with each other. I rejected dropping the pupil from the sheet, and I rejected treating the absence as a zero, which is a penalty the school did not decide to impose.

**27. Marks can be edited only while a result set is in Draft or Returned for Correction.** Approval closes the sheet to editing, and correcting an approved sheet means returning it, which is an audited action with a reason. My reason is that the head teacher's approval means nothing if a mark can move afterwards without anybody knowing. I rejected allowing edits with a warning, and I rejected a separate correction table shadowing the marks, which produces two answers to the question of what the pupil scored.

**28. Publication can be undone only by a Super Admin, and republication is visible to parents.** Withdrawing a published result needs a reason, and a corrected sheet carries an incremented revision number and a notice telling the parent that the sheet has been revised and when. I chose visible revision because the alternative is a parent who screenshots a sheet on Monday, returns on Friday and finds different figures with no explanation, which reads as a school changing marks quietly. I rejected silent republication, and I rejected refusing to correct published sheets, since genuine errors happen.

**29. Subjects are mapped to a level for a term, with per-arm exceptions that need a reason.** The ordinary case, which is that all of Primary 3 takes the same nine subjects, is one action. The exception, which is that 3B takes French and 3A does not because there is one French teacher, is possible and recorded. My reason is that mapping arm by arm makes the common case nine times the work and guarantees drift between arms of the same level. I rejected arm-only mapping and level-only mapping, the first for the workload and the second because the exception is real.

**30. Position is computed within the arm, and the level position is a separate line computed on the average, labelled with its cohort and its size.** A pupil is 3rd of 28 in Primary 3A and 5th of 54 in Primary 3, and the sheet says both in those words. I decided this because a bare "Position: 3rd" on a sheet from a two-arm level invites a parent to compare it against a sheet from the other arm, where 3rd means something different. Computing the level position on the average rather than the total keeps the comparison honest when the two arms have not sat identical subject sets. I rejected an arm position alone, which is what most software prints and what generates the argument, and I rejected replacing it with a level position alone, since the form teacher's ranking is the one the class recognises.

**31. Affective and psychomotor traits are recorded per pupil per term on a five-point scale, and the trait list is configurable.** I chose a fixed-width scale so that the sheet has a predictable layout and the teacher's task is a row of taps rather than free text thirty times. I rejected free-text trait comments, which nobody completes by the thirtieth pupil, and I rejected omitting traits, because these rows are on every Nigerian primary result sheet and their absence would be the first thing the head teacher noticed.

**32. Remarks are typed by people, with a length limit and no generated text.** The class teacher's remark and the head teacher's remark are separate fields, and the head teacher's remark is required before publication. I decided against generating a remark from the average because a sheet that says "Excellent result, keep it up" in identical words for nine children tells the parent exactly how much thought went into it. I rejected a template picker for the same reason, though I accept the school may ask for one later, and Appendix B question 11 puts the question to them.

**33. A pupil who joins mid-session is ranked annually on the terms actually sat, and the sheet says how many.** A child who arrives in the Second Term gets an annual average from two terms, printed with the words "based on 2 of 3 terms". My reason is that averaging in a missing term as zero would place a transferring pupil at the bottom of the class through no fault of their own, and hiding the pupil from the annual ranking would leave the parent with no cumulative figure at all. I rejected both. The school may exclude such pupils from prize-giving by its own judgement, and the annual report shows the term count so that judgement is informed.

**34. There is no teacher performance report.** I decided not to build a view that ranks staff by class average, even though the data would allow it. A class average reflects the pupils in the arm, the subject teachers who are not the form teacher, and how the arms were composed at the start of the session, none of which the number controls for, and a number of that sort will be used in a pay conversation. I rejected building it with a caveat attached, because the caveat does not travel with the printout.

## A.5 Access pins and the parent portal

**35. Pins are not tied to a pupil. This was the school's decision, against my recommendation, and it is recorded here as such.** Any valid pin presented with any registration number opens that pupil's results, and each lookup spends one of the pin's uses. My recommendation was to bind each pin to one pupil at generation, because registration numbers here are sequential and printed on uniform tags, so an unbound pin plus a number anybody can read off a school bag is a browsable school. The school weighed that against the operational cost of binding, which is real: pins cannot be printed before the roster settles, a slip cannot be handed to whoever collects it, a parent with three children needs three slips, and every lost slip is a regeneration rather than a reach into the drawer. It chose the operational gain and accepted the exposure. My job at that point is to build the safest version of what was chosen rather than to keep arguing, so this document specifies unbound pins and adds three things the original design did not need: a low default of 3 maximum uses per batch, which is now the main limit on what a lost slip discloses; automatic suspension of any pin that opens more than three distinct pupils, per 6.9.3; and a usage report that records which pupil every use opened, so the school can say precisely what was seen. I rejected bind-on-first-use as a middle position, because a slip lost between the bursar's desk and the parent's hand binds to whoever finds it. What I ask in exchange is in Appendix B question 7: the risk acceptance the school signs must describe the unbound design in plain words, because signing a paragraph about a bound one would not be consent to anything real.

**36. One use means one viewing session of thirty minutes, not one page load.** Inside the window the parent can move between terms, look at the annual result and download the sheet as often as they like. A failed attempt never consumes a use. My reason is that a parent on a weak connection who reloads a page twice must not lose two of twenty uses, and that counting page loads generates support calls that make the school distrust the whole feature. I rejected counting requests, and I rejected unlimited use, which removes any control over a slip that has been photographed and shared.

**37. Pins are stored as an Argon2id hash plus an indexed keyed hash, with the first four characters in plain text permanently and the full value encrypted for thirty days.** The keyed hash is new since the school chose unbound pins: validation now arrives with a pin and no pupil to narrow the search, so the system needs one indexed lookup rather than a scan of Argon2id comparisons, and the key sits outside the database so the index is useless on its own. The four-character prefix lets the office identify which batch a parent is holding over the telephone without being able to see the rest. The encrypted copy exists so the school can reprint a damaged sheet of slips, and it is purged after thirty days because by then the slips are distributed and the copy is only a liability. I rejected storing pins in plain text, which is how a leaked backup becomes every child's results, and I rejected hashing alone, which makes lookup either slow or impossible now that there is no pupil to filter on.

**38. Every portal failure returns the same message and the same timing.** A wrong registration number, a wrong pin, a revoked pin, an exhausted pin and a pin for a different pupil all produce one sentence, and all responses are padded to a minimum of 400 milliseconds. My reason is that distinguishing between them tells an attacker which registration numbers exist, which is the enumeration this design exists to prevent. The cost is that a parent with a genuine problem gets no diagnosis from the page, so the message names the school office and the telephone number, and the office can see the real reason in the usage report. I rejected specific messages, and I rejected specific messages after a delay, which leaks the same information more slowly.

**39. The pupil's photograph is not on the portal result sheet unless the school switches it on.** I made the default exclusion on minimisation grounds: a sheet downloaded to a phone and forwarded on a messaging application should not carry a child's face beside their name and class. The school can switch it on, and the setting says in plain words what switching it on means. I rejected including it by default, which is what the school will ask for because the paper sheet has a photograph, and I rejected removing the option, since the decision belongs to the school.

**40. Published results stay on the portal indefinitely.** A parent can look up the First Term of 2026/2027 in 2031 with the pin from that session, provided the pin has uses left. My reason is that a former parent asking for an old result is a walk to the office and a search through a filing cabinet today, and this removes that. I rejected expiring results after a term, and I rejected expiring them at the end of the session, both of which would send the parent to the office for the exact document the system already holds.

## A.6 Non-functional decisions

**41. Lists use cursor pagination.** I chose cursors over page numbers because a register that gains a pupil while an administrator is on page three of the list will otherwise push a row from page three to page four and skip it entirely. I rejected offset pagination, which is simpler to build and quietly loses rows.

**42. The score entry sheet is never paginated, and it holds unsaved marks in the browser until a save succeeds.** An arm of thirty pupils and nine subjects loads in one request, and a failed save retries with a visible banner rather than dropping the work. This is the requirement I would defend hardest in the whole document. A teacher who loses forty minutes of typing to a dropped connection will go back to paper that afternoon and will be right to. I rejected paging the sheet, and I rejected saving only on a button press without local retention.

**43. Health fields are optional, and they never reach the portal.** Blood group, genotype and the medical note can be left blank, and no setting exposes them to a parent-facing page, a PDF or the verification page. I chose this because the school will not have parental consent for every child, and because a health field on a document that circulates outside the school is the kind of disclosure that follows a child. I rejected making the fields required, and I rejected putting them on the result sheet, which some paper sheets do.

**44. Closing a session produces a downloadable archive bundle, and closing deletes nothing.** The bundle holds every published sheet as a PDF, the broadsheets as CSV, the register, the promotion decisions and the configuration snapshots, and closed sessions stay fully queryable in the live system. My reason is that the school should be able to keep a copy of a year's results in a form that outlives this application. I rejected archiving by moving data out of the live database, which is how a school ends up unable to answer a question about 2027 in 2029.

**45. Everything printable is A4.** No layout in the product assumes Letter, and no artefact depends on colour to be readable. I decided this because A4 is what the school buys and the sheets will be photocopied in monochrome at a business centre. I rejected a configurable paper size, which doubles the layout testing to serve a case that does not exist here.

## A.7 Decisions made from the reference sample

The school sent a screenshot of another school's online result rather than its own sheet. That is still evidence, and the three entries below record what I did with it. They sit apart from the rest of this appendix because they were made later, against a document I had already written, and I want that order visible rather than folded quietly into the numbering as if it had been there from the start.

**46. The cumulative three-term summary and the promotion status print on the Third Term terminal sheet itself, once the annual computation has run, and not only on a separate annual document.** The reference does exactly this: a pupil reading their Third Term result also sees the year's totals, the cumulative average, the promotion outcome and a pass line for each core subject, all on the one page. I adopted it because a parent who receives one sheet at the end of the year and has to be told a second document exists somewhere else is a parent who telephones the office. The separate Annual Cumulative document in 6.7.10 and on the portal is not removed. It remains the thing a school reprints on its own, for a transfer certificate or a much later request, and it is the only form available where a pupil's final arm differs from where they sat some of the year's terms. I rejected removing the annual sheet and keeping only the inline block, because the inline block depends on the Third Term result set for that one arm and the standalone document is what survives a pupil moving between arms or being looked up years later.

**47. Subject position, class highest and class lowest are left off the pupil-facing sheet by default, and only the class average is shown per subject.** The reference sheet does this, and independently of the reference it is the more defensible default: a bare 91 and 44 beside a child's 64 invites a comparison the sheet has not been asked to make, where a class average of 60.1 gives the parent the same context without naming the extremes. Nothing is lost from the system. All three figures are computed and stored regardless, because the broadsheet in section 10 needs them for the head teacher's own comparison, and a school that wants them on the parent's copy too can turn them on without any change to the data model. I rejected leaving them on by default and letting the school turn them off, on the view that a sheet should default to the less exposing choice and require a decision to add detail, not the other way round.

**48. The trait scale's printed legend defaults to a full descriptive sentence per point, for example Maintains an excellent degree of observable traits, rather than the single word I first seeded, Excellent.** The scale was already numeric by default in 6.2.7, one to five, so the reference did not change the scale type. What it changed is the legend: it prints one sentence per point beside the block instead of a word repeated in a key, and a sentence reads better once, off to the side, than a word would if it had to be repeated in every cell of a grid. I widened `point_label` from 24 to 120 characters in 6.2.7 to fit it. A school that prefers a single word can type one; the field takes either without any other change.
