## Goal

A privileged request that should be refused is refused with 403, even when writing the rejection
audit row fails. Today the audit write is awaited with no `try`/`catch`, so a transient database
fault converts a clean authorisation denial into a 500 — the caller learns the endpoint exists and
broke, instead of that they are not allowed, and the 500 masks the denial in the logs.

## Contract delta

None. See header.

## Why this is real

`PrivilegeAuthorizationHandler.cs:73` awaits `auditSink.RecordRejectionAsync(...)` and only then
calls `context.Fail()`. Neither that call site nor
`SystemAuditSink.RecordRejectionAsync` (`backend/src/SchoolManagement.Infrastructure/Audit/SystemAuditSink.cs:48-54`)
wraps the write. `RejectedAuditEventWriter.WriteAsync` deliberately commits on its **own
short-lived connection** (2026-09-09 human sign-off), which is what makes the row durable — and
also means it has its own independent way to fail, separate from the request's transaction.

Reached via `SystemAuditSink.RecordRejectionAsync` and its caller; the same shape may exist at the
other `RecordRejectionAsync` call sites (`UpdateAdminAccountHandler.cs:80`,
`CreateRoleAssignmentHandler.cs:69` and `:178`) — check each, fix what shares the defect.

## The open question this card must NOT decide alone

**Is a rejection that could not be audited allowed to proceed as a 403?**

- Fail-closed (keep the 500) preserves "no denial goes unrecorded", consistent with the
  append-only sign-off.
- Fail-open (403 plus a loud log) preserves the correct security answer to the caller and does not
  let the audit subsystem become an availability lever on authorisation.

Spec 6.1.12 does not settle it. **Ask the human before implementing**, then record the answer as a
decision in `.agent/decisions/2026-Q3.md`. Do not pick one silently — either choice is defensible
and the wrong one is a security-relevant surprise.
