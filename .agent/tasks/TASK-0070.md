## Goal

A subject exists, is mapped to class levels for a term, and an arm may include or exclude one by
exception. The computation engine resolves "the subjects in effect for this arm this term" through
exactly one query, and that resolution is this card's real deliverable.

## The resolution rule, stated once

§8.1 defines it: **level mappings for the term, plus the arm's include exceptions, minus its
exclude exceptions.** Build that as a single resolver the engine calls. Two arms of one level may
legitimately end up with different subject sets — that is why §8.4.7 ranks level position on
average rather than on total, and the engine depends on this card getting it right.

## How the 14/19 split is applied — `POST /subject-mappings/prefill`

Seeding `subject_mapping` is impossible: it needs a `term_id`, and no session or term is seeded. So
the split cannot arrive with the migration. **Human ruling 2026-09-16: a prefill endpoint.** A full
grid is 156 rows (14 × 3 nursery + 19 × 6 primary); hand-ticking that before the school can enter a
mark was rejected, as was applying it automatically on term creation (that leaks §6.6 behaviour into
§6.3 and creates 156 rows with no click).

`POST /subject-mappings/prefill` — see the approved delta, Amendment 5, for the full shape. In
short: **additive only**, never ends a mapping, never duplicates an existing active one; `dryRun`
and the same `SaveSubjectMappingGridResponse` envelope as the grid save and copy; `Subject.Map`
alone (no `Unmap` — it cannot end anything); `Idempotency-Key` required.

The standard list is a **backend constant**, not a new table and not stored config — it is a
starting point the administrator edits afterwards through the ordinary grid, exactly like a copy.
Resolve each subject to levels by the level's `SectionId`: `N` rows to the three Nursery levels,
`P` rows to the six Primary levels, `B` rows to all nine.

### `display_order` comes from the SHEET order, not the seed table

The seed table above is grouped `N`/`B`/`P` for readability. **That is not the printed row order.**
`display_order` is "row order of the subject on the result sheet for that level" (§6.6.3), and the
sheets have their own order — reproduced here from appendices E and F, and confirmed against the
school's own two sheets. Prefill must write `display_order` from these, per section:

**Nursery (14, in order):** Number work · Letter work · Phonics · Quantitative Reasoning · Verbal
Reasoning · Literature · Pre Science · Social habit · Health habit · Handwriting · Christian
Religious Knowledge · Computer Science · Creative skills · Rhyme

**Primary (19, in order):** Mathematics · English Language · Phonics/Diction · Quantitative
Reasoning · Verbal Reasoning · Literature · Hand writing · Basic Science/Tech · Social Studies ·
Health Education · Christian Religious Knowledge · Computer Science · Creative Art · Agric Science ·
Home Economics · Civic Education · History · French · Igbo

`display_order` is therefore a property of the **mapping**, not of the subject — one subject can sit
at a different row on each section's sheet.

As it happens all five shared subjects land on the SAME position in both lists (Quantitative
Reasoning 4, Verbal Reasoning 5, Literature 6, Christian Religious Knowledge 11, Computer Science
12) — the school evidently built both sheets from one template. **Do not turn that into a shortcut.**
It is a property of today's two lists, not a rule; the moment either sheet is reordered it stops
holding, and a `display_order` derived from the subject rather than from the (subject, section)
mapping would silently print the other section's order. Key it off the level's section, always.

**Three near-duplicate pairs are seeded as separate rows on purpose** — Handwriting/Hand writing,
Phonics/Phonics-Diction, Creative skills/Creative Art. Each appendix reproduces names as the school
wrote them, and merging is a school decision. Recorded as drift; do not merge them yourself.

## Why `code` is optional

§6.6.2 marks `code` required and says it is "the column header on the broadsheet where the full name
will not fit". Four facts against seeding one:

1. **Nothing prints a code today.** The arm broadsheet (`15-reporting-requirements.md`,
   `GET /result-sets/{id}/broadsheet`) is unbuilt and sits past TASK-0071. The two termly report
   sheets print full subject names down the side — checked against the school's own documents.
2. **The one data contract that names it declares it OPTIONAL.**
   `18-appendix-c-result-sheet-contract.md:47` — `subject_code | Snapshot, optional`. Its
   `subject_name` neighbour on line 45 carries no such qualifier. §6.6.2's `Req: Yes` is the outlier.
3. **The school has no code concept.** Neither sheet carries one. §6.6.2's "ENG, MTH, BST" are the
   spec author's examples, not the school's data — so any seeded value is invented.
4. **§6.6.2 is the section already proven to carry superseded text** (see the was/now table above).
   Deferring to it over appendix C would be deferring to the less reliable of the two.

Nullable costs nothing later: the broadsheet card can populate codes from the school, or render a
truncated name. Required-and-invented would have put 28 fabricated strings in the seed and a
`409 subject.code_duplicate` on a field nobody supplies.
