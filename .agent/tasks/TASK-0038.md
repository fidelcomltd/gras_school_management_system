## Why it matters beyond its own module

Three things are waiting on this card, which is why it comes next among the backend cards:

1. **TASK-0030** (role assignments) needs `arm_ids` to exist before a scope can reference them.
   With TASK-0035 landed, this card is the *only* remaining blocker on it — and TASK-0030 is what
   finally replaces `SuperAdminFlagEffectivePrivilegeProvider`, the standing authorization bypass.
2. **TASK-0035 left two accepted drifts** whose owner is this card (both in `STATE.md`
   `## Known drift`): `POST /terms/{id}/open` currently does **not** enforce spec 6.3.6's "at
   least one arm exists for the session" precondition, so opening a term is more permissive than
   spec; and the session list/detail omit spec 6.3.8's arm counts. Both seams are marked
   `DEFERRED` in `OpenTermHandler.cs:16` and `SessionDto.cs:35` — **this card resolves them and
   rewrites those comments**, rather than leaving them to be rediscovered.
3. `section` (spec 06 §6.4) drives result-sheet routing and is load-bearing, not descriptive —
   spec 15 and appendices 21/22 read it.

## Scope sketch (expand before dispatch)

Read spec `product-specification/06-module-class-levels-arms.md` §6.4 in full before writing the
card; the notes below are a pointer, not a substitute.

- Class level entity with the progression chain (which level follows which) — the chain is what
  promotion later walks, so it is ordered data, not a label.
- Arm entity belonging to a **level and a session**, with capacity. Spec 6.4.9's over-capacity
  rule **warns, never blocks** — TASK-0036 depends on that reading.
- The `section` field (nursery / primary), driving result-sheet routing.
- Privileges: `level.*` and `arm.*` — check `Domain/Security/Privileges.cs` first; the seeded
  roles in `SeededRoles.cs` already reference `arm.view` and `level.view`, so the constants very
  likely already exist and must not be re-invented.
- Arm deletion interacts with TASK-0030's assignment cascade (spec 6.1.13 revokes an emptied
  assignment). Whichever of the two lands second owns that cascade; note which in the card.
